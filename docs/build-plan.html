Dualtone

HBS × MIT Hackathon · Saturday, October 24 · build 12:00 → submit by 3:30 PM · demo 3:45

Build plan for a two-lane front desk, in three hours, on Replit

Dualtone answers a plumber’s phone and sorts every caller into one of three kinds. People ring through to the owner; if he’s on a job, a disclosed assistant takes a message and promises a callback time. Booking agents post a refundable 5¢ hold, get a two-second structured lane, and pay a $15 deposit in USDC on Monad. Sales and spam bots won’t pay the hold, so they never reach the owner. This page holds every decision, the task split, the prompts to paste into Replit Agent, and the 90-second script.

Hero customerPlumbing & HVAC shops with under 20 staff (96,222 US firms). Demo business: “Rivera Plumbing & Heating”.
The insightAn agent shopping five plumbers books the first one it can read. Agent-readable is the new “answering the phone”.
Three kinds of callersHuman, booking agent, sales or spam agent. The first decision on every call. When unsure, treat the caller as human.
Humans reach a humanRing the owner first. If missed: a disclosed assistant takes a message, promises a callback time and texts it. It never books or quotes for people.
Why MonadA 5¢ refundable hold for every agent: good agents get it back, spam pays. On cards the ~30¢ fixed fee makes that impossible.
Deposits & priorityAgents only pay a $15 deposit. The owner’s rules decide what agents may commit to and which slots stay with people.
DisclosureMassachusetts requires every party’s consent to record, so the greeting says it’s an assistant and that the call is transcribed.
Voice & AISimulated call in the browser, text plus browser speech. LLM through Replit AI Integrations, or the free Gemini API as backup.

Architecture

Two Repls, so you can both build at once, and the agent-to-agent exchange is real

Replit allows one Agent task at a time per project, and two people prompting one Repl collide. So each of you owns one Repl, and the two meet at a small, fixed contract. The split is also true to the product: the front desk belongs to the plumber, while the caller agent and its wallet belong to the homeowner’s assistant.

The contract between the two Repls (agree on this at 12:10, then don’t change it)

Repl A (front desk) exposes:
  GET  /.well-known/agent-card.json      A2A-style Agent Card
  POST /a2a/hello  body {"agent","on_behalf_of","intent"}      intent: "book_service" | "sell_services"
       no header            → 402 {"classification":"booking_agent" | "sales_agent",
                                   "payment_required":{"amount_usd":0.05,"purpose":"refundable_hold",
                                   "asset":"USDC","network":"eip155:10143","pay_to":"<BUSINESS_ADDRESS>"}}
       header X-PAYMENT-TX  → 200 {"session_id":"S-…","classification":"booking_agent"}
  POST /a2a/book   body {"session_id","issue","zip","window","max_price_usd"}
       no header            → 402 {"offer":{"slot":"Today 4:00 PM","diagnostic_fee_usd":89,
                                   "policy_note":"2:00 PM is held for human callers by the owner's rules"},
                                   "payment_required":{"amount_usd":15,"purpose":"deposit",
                                   "asset":"USDC","network":"eip155:10143","pay_to":"<BUSINESS_ADDRESS>"}}
       header X-PAYMENT-TX  → 200 {"booking_id":"RPH-1042","slot":"Today 4:00 PM","status":"confirmed",
                                   "hold_refund":{"amount_usd":0.05}}

Repl B (caller agent) exposes:
  POST /api/caller/run  body {"frontDeskUrl"} → {"steps":[{"tMs","from","to","label","json"}],
                                                 "payments":[{"purpose","hash","explorerUrl","settledMs"}],
                                                 "booking":{...}, "totalMs"}
  POST /api/reset       sends the test USDC back to the caller wallet (run before every demo)

In the demo Repl B signs for both wallets, so it also sends the 5¢ refund.
In production the hold would sit in an escrow contract that Dualtone controls.
Explorer link format: https://testnet.monadvision.com/tx/<hash>

Steps:

Be ready

Likely judge questions

Why Monad and not Stripe?

The 5-cent refundable hold. A card payment carries about 30¢ in fixed fees, so a 5¢ hold that is refunded on every legitimate call can’t exist on cards. On Monad it costs a fraction of a cent and settles in under a second. Spam bots that won’t pay never reach the owner, and every hold leaves a record of which agents pay, book and show up. Deposits could also run on cards later; the hold can’t.

How do you detect agents that don’t say they’re agents?

Three signals: an agent card handshake, disclosure (Google’s agent announces itself at the start of the call), and a classifier on the first lines. The errors aren’t equal, so when confidence is below 0.8 we treat the caller as human: a bot reaching the owner is annoying, a person reaching a bot breaks our promise. The owner can mark any call “this was a person”, and we report the misclassification rate on the dashboard.

Doesn’t screening add friction for people?

There is no screening question and no phone tree. Classification is passive, from the first seconds of the call. Humans ring straight through to the owner; the assistant only picks up when he can’t.

Can the AI commit the business to something?

Only within the owner’s rules: published prices, open slots that aren’t held for people, and the $15 deposit. Custom quotes, jobs over $500 and after-hours emergencies need the owner’s approval. In the product, availability comes from the live calendar, so the agent never guesses.

What about recording and AI-disclosure laws?

Massachusetts and California require every party’s consent to record. The greeting says it is an automated assistant and that the call is transcribed, and that is built in from the first version.

Is holding stablecoins a money-transmission problem?

Possibly, which is why in production the hold and deposits would run through a licensed stablecoin payments provider or an escrow contract, not through our own wallet. Today’s demo uses testnet funds.

Doesn’t a fast lane for agents favor people who can afford an AI assistant?

It would if speed meant priority, so it doesn’t. The owner sets the booking rules: hold same-day slots for human callers, put urgent callers first whatever the channel. Only agents put down a deposit, because they shop many plumbers at once and can no-show at scale. People who call never pay up front. In a 2026 Lorikeet survey, booking was the one task where more consumers were uncomfortable than comfortable with an AI acting for them, so the human lane is the product too, not a fallback.

Why would a homeowner pay in crypto?

They don’t. Human callers never pay anything up front. Only agents post the hold and the $15 deposit, from their own wallet in USDC, a dollar stablecoin, and the plumber sees dollars. Agents can’t click a texted payment link, so they need a payment they can make themselves.

Why won’t Google or Yelp just build this, or Goodcall add an “agent mode”?

Google, OpenAI, Visa and Coinbase build rails: A2A, AP2, ACP, x402. None of them answers a three-person shop’s phone or holds its calendar. An incumbent receptionist could add an agent mode, so our moat is what’s hard to copy: detection accuracy, measured and corrected by owners, and the reputation data from holds about which agents pay, book and show up.

If Gemini calls from the customer’s number, how do you detect it?

Google’s agent announces that it is automated at the start of the call, and agents that support A2A can fetch our agent card. We also run a classifier on the first lines. Agents have a reason to identify themselves: the fast lane books them faster.

How many agent calls do plumbers actually get?

Nobody publishes that number yet, which is part of the opportunity. Google has phoned US businesses for prices since July 2025 and added more categories in 2026. Our pilot will measure it, and the dashboard counts it from day one.

What do you charge, and why would an owner buy?

$199 a month, flat, cancel anytime, plus 0.5% on deposits; holds kept from spam bots go to the owner. The top complaints about current AI receptionists are surprise billing and cancellation traps, so simple pricing is part of the product. Why buy: 44% of callers never reach a person, a missed call goes to the next plumber, and the owner stops losing his day to sales bots. Our validation calls today, and the pilot, should put a dollar figure on a missed job before we claim one.

Research behind the deck

What we found, and where it came from

Competitors (checked October 24)

  • 14 SMB AI receptionists (Goodcall, Slang.ai, Smith.ai, Ruby, Rosie, Dialzara, Upfirst, My AI Front Desk, Numa, Podium, Weave, Yelp, ServiceTitan, and others): none treats AI agents as a separate, machine-readable channel.
  • Deposits in the call: only Smith.ai and Ruby, by hand; Weave says “coming soon”. Venues, restaurants and vets take deposits by web form or texted link.
  • Pindrop BotStopper (Sep 2026) detects AI callers, but for enterprises, and it blocks them.
  • Top complaints in 1–3 star reviews: billing and cancellation traps, slow support, older callers hanging up on bots.

Why now

  • Google “Ask for Me” / AI calling: rolled out to all US users on July 16, 2025; plumbers among the businesses called.
  • Gemini “Call for Me”: reported September 24, 2026, dials from the user’s own number.
  • Standards: A2A v1.0 (Linux Foundation), AP2 (now at FIDO), ACP (OpenAI and Stripe, beta), x402 (Coinbase) with a Monad facilitator supporting testnet.
  • Monad mainnet since November 24, 2025; finality about 600 ms; a USDC transfer costs a fraction of a cent.
